Understanding Variance

Mean tells us where data are centered. Variance tells us how far the observations spread out from that center.

August 31, 2026•Ahamed Learning Notes

Variance is one of the most important concepts in statistics and machine learning. It answers a simple question:

How far are my data points spread out from the average?

1. The intuition behind variance

Imagine two classes of students with different exam scores.

Class A

70, 72, 68, 71, 69
μ = 70

The scores cluster closely around their average.

Class B

40, 60, 70, 80, 110
μ = 72

The average is similar, but the scores are much more spread out. Variance captures this difference:

  • Class A: low variance.
  • Class B: high variance.

2. How do we calculate variance?

  1. Find the mean.
  2. Calculate how far each value is from the mean.
  3. Square each of those differences.
  4. Average the squared differences.

The difference between an observation and the mean is called its deviation.

Worked example

Consider the data:

2, 4, 6

Step 1: calculate the mean

μ = (2 + 4 + 6) / 3 = 4

Step 2: calculate the deviations

ValueDeviation from mean
22 - 4 = -2
44 - 4 = 0
66 - 4 = 2

Why not average the deviations?

(-2 + 0 + 2) / 3 = 0

Deviations below and above the mean cancel one another. In fact, deviations from the mean always sum to zero, so their ordinary average cannot measure spread. Squaring removes the negative signs.

Step 3: square the deviations

ValueDeviationSquared deviation
2-24
400
624

Step 4: average the squared deviations

Variance = (4 + 0 + 4) / 3 = 8 / 3 ≈ 2.67

3. Population variance

When the data contain every member of the population, population variance is written as:

σ2 = ∑(xi - μ)2 N
  • σ2 is the population variance.
  • xi is each observation.
  • μ is the population mean.
  • N is the number of observations in the population.

4. Sample variance

Often we cannot measure an entire population. For example, rather than measuring the height of every human on Earth, we might study a sample of 1,000 people. Sample variance is written as:

s2 = ∑(xi - x̄)2 n - 1

Population variance divides by N. Sample variance divides by n - 1.

Why do we use n - 1?

This adjustment is called Bessel's correction. When the sample mean is used in place of the unknown population mean, dividing by n tends to underestimate population variance. Dividing by n - 1 corrects that bias.

Remember: use n - 1 when estimating population variance from a sample.

5. The units of variance

Because deviations are squared, variance is expressed in squared units. If distance is measured in meters, variance is measured in square meters. If height is measured in centimeters, variance is measured in square centimeters. 

Data:      centimeters (cm)
Variance: square centimeters (cm²)

Squared units can be difficult to interpret. That is why we often use standard deviation, the square root of variance, which returns to the original unit.

6. Variance in machine learning

A. The bias-variance tradeoff

A model can fail in two important ways.

High bias

A high-bias model is too simple to learn the underlying pattern. This produces underfitting.

High variance

A high-variance model responds too strongly to the particular training set. It may memorize training examples but fail to generalize to new data. This produces overfitting. 

Low bias + high variance
           |
           v
       Overfitting

Understanding variance is therefore central to understanding model generalization.

B. Feature selection

Consider a feature with little variation:

Age: 25, 26, 27, 28, 29

Now consider a feature with much greater variation:

Income: $20k, $50k, $100k, $200k, $500k

The income feature may contain more distinguishing information. Some feature-selection methods remove near-zero-variance features, although variance alone does not prove that a feature is useful.

C. Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Imagine viewing a stretched cloud of data points. PCA rotates the coordinate system so its first axis follows the longest direction of that cloud—the direction containing the most variance.

Projecting the points onto PC1 preserves more of their differences than projecting them onto another direction. This is why PCA can reduce dimensions while retaining much of the information.

7. Variance in Python

import numpy as np

data = np.array([2, 4, 6])

population_variance = np.var(data)
sample_variance = np.var(data, ddof=1)

print(population_variance)
print(sample_variance)

Output:

2.6666666666666665
4.0

Rounded to two decimal places, the population variance is 2.67. Setting ddof=1 makes NumPy divide by n - 1, producing the sample variance of 4.0.

9. Mean vs. variance

ConceptWhat it tells us
MeanWhere the data are centered
VarianceHow spread out the data are

Same mean, different distributions

Dataset A: 48, 49, 50, 51, 52
Mean: 50
Variance: small

Dataset B: 10, 30, 50, 70, 90
Mean: 50
Variance: large

The two datasets share the same mean but have completely different distributions. A measure of center alone cannot tell the full story.

Key idea: the mean locates the center; variance measures the average squared distance from that center.
