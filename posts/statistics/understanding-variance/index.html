Understanding Variance Beyond the Formula

Variance measures how far values spread around their mean, but the formula becomes useful only when you understand what each part is doing.

July 12, 2026•Ahamed Learning Notes

Suppose two datasets have the same mean. They can still behave very differently. One may stay tightly grouped around the center while the other spreads far away. Variance gives us a precise language for that difference.

Start with distance from the mean

For every value, subtract the mean. The result is its deviation. Positive deviations sit above the mean; negative deviations sit below it.

σ² = (1 / N) ∑ᵢ₌₁ᴺ (xᵢ − μ)²

Adding raw deviations will always cancel to zero. Squaring prevents that cancellation and gives more influence to large deviations.

A small example

For the values 2, 4, and 6, the mean is 4. Their deviations are −2, 0, and 2. Squaring produces 4, 0, and 4, so the population variance is 8/3.

Keep this intuition: variance is the average squared distance from the mean. Standard deviation converts that squared distance back to the original unit.

Why it matters

Variance appears in uncertainty estimates, feature scaling, PCA, regression, probability distributions, and many optimization objectives.
